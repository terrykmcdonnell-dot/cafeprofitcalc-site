How to calculate café revenue

A café's revenue comes down to two numbers for each part of the day: how many customers you serve, and what each one spends. Seats matter less than in a restaurant, because most café trade is takeaway or quick sit-down, and the real limit is how fast you can serve the morning queue.

Weekly revenue = (customers × average sale, for each part of the day) × days open, plus catering and other sales

Work each part of the day out separately. A morning coffee-and-go customer and a lunch customer having a toastie and a drink spend very different amounts, and one blended average hides where the money actually comes from.

A worked example

The calculator above opens with this example: a suburban café open six days a week, with a strong morning trade, a steady lunch and a quiet afternoon.

Time of dayWorkingPer day
Morning120 customers × $8.50$1,020
Lunch50 customers × $16$800
Afternoon30 customers × $6$180
Daily total200 customers, $10 average sale$2,000

Six days gives $12,000 a week, plus $500 of catering and retail beans makes $12,500 a week. Over 50 trading weeks that's $625,000 a year. Notice that the afternoon, open for perhaps three hours, brings in less than a tenth of the day's sales while still needing staff, power and a full cabinet.

Three ways to grow café revenue

1. Lift the average sale

Every customer who adds a pastry, upsizes or buys a second coffee for a colleague lifts revenue without making the queue any longer. Adding 50 cents to the average sale in the example is worth $30,000 a year. Our guide on how to increase café revenue without more customers covers the practical ways to do it.

2. Fill the quiet hours, not the busy ones

Most cafés can't serve many more people at 8am: a barista can only make so many coffees an hour, and a long queue sends people elsewhere. The afternoon is where there's spare capacity. An afternoon offer, a meeting-friendly space or a school-pickup trade can add customers at a time you're already paying staff to be there.

3. Trade more days, carefully

Opening a seventh day adds a full day's sales, but also a full day's wages, often at weekend or holiday rates. It only pays if that day's sales cover its own costs. The break-even calculator helps you test that before you roster for it.

To see these levers clearly you need sales and transactions broken down by hour of the day. Most modern POS systems report this automatically; a system like TouchBistro shows transactions and average sale by hour without you building a spreadsheet. Forecasting revenue for a new café

The calculator works for a café that hasn't opened yet, but be honest with the inputs. New owners nearly always overestimate customer numbers, especially in the first six months while locals are still finding them.

  • Count the foot traffic past your site at 7–9am on a weekday before you sign the lease, and assume only a small share of it will stop.
  • Run the numbers at the customers you expect, then again at 20% fewer. If the lower figure doesn't cover your costs, the plan is fragile.
  • Base the average sale on cafés like yours nearby, not on your menu prices. Plenty of morning customers buy one coffee and nothing else.

Revenue isn't profit

A café can take $625,000 a year and still struggle to pay its owner. Revenue tells you the size of the business; coffee and food cost, wages and rent decide how much of it you keep. Once you know your revenue, the café profit calculator shows your real margin, and what a good café profit margin looks like puts it in context.

Frequently asked questions

How do you calculate café revenue?

For each part of the day, multiply customers by average sale, add them together, and multiply by the days you open. Add catering, retail and other sales on top.

What is average transaction value?

Sales divided by transactions. It counts each sale at the till, so a group paying together counts once. Your POS reports it directly.

What grows café revenue fastest?

If your morning rush is already at capacity, a higher average sale or more afternoon trade. If you have spare capacity all day, more customers. The table in the calculator shows the annual effect of each on your own numbers.

Should café revenue include GST or sales tax?

No. Use sales excluding GST, VAT or sales tax. The tax isn't your money, and including it makes every cost percentage look better than it is.

How do I find my numbers by time of day?

Run your POS sales report by hour for two or three typical weeks, then add up the hours into morning, lunch and afternoon. A single week, or one sunny Saturday, will give you a misleading picture.
